## More recently,
<br>
I've been on the offensive, presenting the work to all who wish to hear and see what we've been doing as a team. It's often a thing I push my team to do, instead of "pull" we also "push" communication back - invite the audience into our world and share what we've been doing and why. 

My presentations tend to be quite structured and land (usually!), it looks something like:

<ul>
<li><strong>Shape why you are here and what to expect from this presentation</strong> <br><em>This sets the scene and checks that they even want to be here</em></li><br>
<li><strong>This design is MVP AND a very relaxed presentation, nudge me if you want to ask a question, anytime, and I'll happily stop</strong><br><em>This lets everyone, we all own this, not just the designer and developers</em>
</li><br>
<li><strong>User needs AND business needs, what are they, what did they say</strong><br><em>I show quotes, always with the quotes - it puts "real" to it!</em></li><br>
<li><strong>Summarise the problems, what are we going to tackle (it's not everything)</strong><br><em>Set the scene for the next stage of the presentation..</em></li><br>
<li><strong>Show the visuals, walk through journeys</strong><br><em>I use the 'jobs to be done' model to present this: no solo screens, but a reason why the user is doing a thing - usually from from login to submit. This helps the user 'feel in the design' - thanks to <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/david-evans-42653813/">David Evans</a> for that nudge!</em></li><br>
<li><strong>Provide space for questions, it's a simple one, but I've seen so many presentations run the full 30 minutes or so, I always plan 10 minutes at least for next steps, and Q and A.</strong></li>

</ul>

All the presentations have landed, they've ranged from the actual end users to very senior stakeholders - I often call these meetings "keeping the lights on", and I think I've managed to get a new candle to the lanteen!

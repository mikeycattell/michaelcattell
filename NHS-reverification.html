NHS Digital

Improving patient contact accuracy across NHS services.

Leading design for a national NHS service helping citizens review and update their contact details, supporting more accurate patient records across healthcare services.

Role

Lead Interaction & Service Designer

Organisation

NHS Digital

Stage

Discovery → Alpha

Scale

10+ million citizens

Team I led

Content design, service design and 2 user researchers

Collaboration

NHS Login identity & verification teams

The challenge.

NHS Digital identified a major issue with the accuracy of patient contact information. Millions of records contained outdated or missing email addresses and phone numbers, affecting how services communicate with patients.

The programme aimed to create a national service allowing citizens to securely review and update their details, but delivery had already begun before the service had a clear user-centred design foundation.

At the point I joined, discovery work had progressed without strong user validation, two consultancies were working together with different scopes of work and stakeholders were focused heavily on deadlines and delivery momentum.

Leading the programme.

I was brought in through Hippo Digital to lead interaction and service design across the programme.

While my official title was Lead Interaction Designer, the role involved guiding the overall design approach, supporting the wider design team and rebuilding stakeholder confidence in the programme direction.

I introduced a structured approach to identifying assumptions and risks, linking them directly to user needs and delivery decisions. This helped move conversations away from opinion-driven decisions and towards evidence-based discussions.

  • Led interaction and service design across Discovery and Alpha.
  • Managed and supported a small multidisciplinary design team.
  • Facilitated alignment workshops across delivery teams.
  • Created a shared roadmap connecting design work and delivery milestones.
  • Worked closely with NHS Login identity and verification teams.

Designing the service.

Alongside stabilising the programme, I led the design of the citizen experience.

The goal was to create a simple and accessible journey allowing patients to review and update their contact details with confidence.

  • Mapped the end-to-end service journey.
  • Simplified fragmented workflows across NHS systems.
  • Designed accessible user flows for a wide range of users.
  • Developed prototypes for testing and stakeholder decision-making.
  • Aligned the service with NHS design patterns and identity flows.

Prototypes became an important tool for helping stakeholders understand the service and support informed decisions about product direction.

Outcomes.

The programme successfully passed Discovery and Alpha service assessments, establishing a clear path for continued delivery.

  • A validated service design addressing a major patient data challenge.
  • A working prototype demonstrating the service end-to-end.
  • Improved collaboration between design, delivery and policy teams.
  • Increased stakeholder confidence in the programme direction.
  • A roadmap supporting continued development of the service.

The work helped move the programme from an unstable early phase to a stronger foundation for delivery.

Looking back.

This project reinforced how important design leadership can be within complex delivery environments.

When delivery pressure is high and teams are misaligned, design can play a critical role in connecting user needs with operational realities.

In this programme, the role extended beyond designing interfaces. A large part of the work involved stabilising the team, rebuilding trust in design and helping the programme move forward with greater clarity and confidence.
